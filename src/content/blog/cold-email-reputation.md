I've worked with quite a lot of firms at this point, and their first concern is usually the safety of their reputation. As an advisor or a private equity firm, you spend a lot of time building your reputation, and you definitely don't want to put a stain on it.

We worked with one client where, when we got on a call, they asked me every question you can think of: how we protect their reputation, their name, their domain. All of those are valid questions.

But I want to call out one statement in particular: "Cold email will ruin my reputation." I hear it all the time on calls, and I'd say it's a myth.

## Stop overrating your last email

As humans, we think from our own perspective. We think we're the center of the world, and that every email we send gets a lot of attention and everybody remembers what we said and who said it.

Think about the last 10 emails you got yesterday. You probably don't remember what most of them said or who sent them, unless one of them was some ridiculous message.

My point is that people aren't as concerned with what you say, and they don't remember you as much as you think they do.

## Avoid the three things that stick

That said, cold email won't ruin your reputation as long as you do it right. Obviously there are some things to avoid. If you use profanity, ridiculous language or made-up claims, then yes, you'll most likely get called out.

## Expect a few angry replies

Sometimes people will get angry that you emailed them, and you have to chalk that up as part of the game. It's part of the business. Someone might be having a bad day, or might be annoyed by other emails. That has nothing to do with you, and you shouldn't take it personally. It's just part of trying to grow your business.

## Never send from your main domain

One way we protect our clients, for extra peace of mind, is that we never use their main domain for sending. Say your website is google.com. We never use google.com to land in someone's inbox. We use alternative domains, separate from your main one, so your reputation stays fully protected.

There is a technical reason too. Gmail counts all mail from the same primary domain together, subdomains included, and treats anyone sending close to 5,000 or more a day to Gmail as a bulk sender. Since May 2025, Outlook has required domains sending over 5,000 a day to pass SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and mail that fails goes to junk first and is then rejected.

If you're doing outreach yourself, I'd still advise getting a few general domains that are not similar to your main domain. You don't want to get blacklisted because you have lots of domains with similar names. That's a common cold email pattern. So do it for durability, for staying out of spam and for your own peace of mind.

Keep them general, advisory-type or finance-type. You can redirect them to your website.

## Buy your domains in batches

If you want 10, 20 or 30 domains, don't buy them all at once. Spread it out: five today, seven tomorrow, three the day after. That protects you from being fingerprinted.

## Send 15 a day and stop at three

To avoid spam and keep your mailboxes healthy, I'd recommend about 15 emails a day per mailbox.

It's also important not to be overly persistent with follow-ups, because no answer is an answer. We keep our sequences to two steps, three at most. If someone isn't interested, they get two or three emails max, and that's it. We don't keep following up.

I've heard some advisors say they run five-step or seven-step sequences, which is overkill. Not only will you annoy the person you're emailing, you'll most likely get yourself flagged as a spammer, and your emails will start going to spam.

Gmail asks senders to keep the share of people who mark them as spam below 0.1%, and never let it reach 0.3%. Every extra follow-up to someone who already ignored you twice is another chance to land in that number.

## Keep the first email plain text

Don't include booking links or website links in the first email. Once they reply, you can send the link, but keep the first emails plain text only.

## Set it up yourself

If you decide to do it yourself, this is what I'd recommend:

1. **Domains.** Get a few domains as I described, not your main domain. Keep them general, advisory-type or finance-type, and redirect them to your website.
2. **Buy in batches.** Five today, seven tomorrow, three the day after.
3. **Sequencer.** Use a sequencer like Smartlead or Instantly for the first batch you send.
4. **Mailboxes.** If you go with Instantly or Smartlead, they also offer their own mailbox setup, so you don't have to do the technical work. They set everything up for you. You build the list, plug it in and start sending.
5. **Lead source.** I'd avoid Apollo, because everyone on Earth and their mother is emailing people from Apollo. If you email those contacts, you'll most likely get no response at all, or a negative one.

That's the easiest way to get started, even for sending a single email.

## Build the list before the copy

The main thing I'll also say: if you're in mergers and acquisitions or private equity, list quality matters far more than the script. Take your time and build a proper list, and only then think about the copy. With the right list, you can write really good, personalized and relevant copy.
